How long does a Lasting Power of Attorney take?

Updated November 2026

A Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) lets people you trust make decisions for you if you can't. It only works once it has been registered, so it's worth understanding how long that takes.

The two types of LPA

  • Property and Financial Affairs: paying bills, managing bank accounts, dealing with pensions and benefits, and selling property.
  • Health and Welfare: decisions about medical treatment, care and where you live. This one can only be used once you've lost the ability to make the decision yourself.

Most people make both, and each one is registered separately.

The steps

  1. Choose your attorneys. These are the people who'll make decisions for you. You can have more than one and decide whether they act together or separately.
  2. Choose a certificate provider. This is someone who confirms you understand the LPA and aren't being pressured into making it.
  3. Sign in the right order. You sign first, then the certificate provider, then your attorneys. Getting the order wrong is one of the most common reasons an LPA is rejected.
  4. Register with the Office of the Public Guardian. The LPA can't be used until this is done.

How long registration takes

Once the Office of the Public Guardian receives a correctly completed application, it checks the paperwork and gives anyone who's been notified a chance to object. Processing times change throughout the year and have ranged from a couple of months to several months in recent years. If there's a mistake, the application is sent back and you lose time correcting it.

The safest approach is to make your LPA well before you think you'll need it, and to have it checked carefully before it's sent.

What it costs to register

The Office of the Public Guardian charges £92 to register each LPA, so £184 if you make both types. You may be able to pay less, or nothing, depending on your income. Check the current fee and exemptions on GOV.UK before you apply.

Why you shouldn't wait

You can only make an LPA while you have the mental capacity to understand it. If illness or an accident takes that away first, your family may have to apply to the Court of Protection to be appointed as your deputy. That process is usually slower, more expensive and comes with ongoing supervision.

How we help

We talk through who you'd like as your attorneys, prepare the documents, guide everyone through signing in the right order and check everything before it's sent for registration. That gives your LPA the best chance of being registered first time.
