Your questions answered

Can't see your question here? Give us a call and we will be happy to help.

I'm married. Do I still need a will?

Yes. Without a will, your husband, wife or civil partner may not inherit everything, especially if you have children. A will makes sure your estate is shared exactly as you want.

My partner and I aren't married. What happens if one of us dies?

Unmarried partners have no automatic right to inherit under the intestacy rules, however long you have been together. Making wills is the simplest way to protect each other.

What is the difference between a will and a Lasting Power of Attorney?

A will only takes effect after you die. A Lasting Power of Attorney covers your lifetime, letting people you trust make decisions if you are unable to make them yourself. Most people benefit from having both.

When should I make a Lasting Power of Attorney?

While you are fit and well. An LPA can only be made while you have the mental capacity to do so. If you wait until it is needed, it may be too late.

How often should I review my will?

We suggest every three to five years, and always after a big life event such as marriage, divorce, the birth of a child or grandchild, or buying a new home. Remember that getting married cancels any existing will in England and Wales.

Do I need a trust?

Not everyone does. Trusts can be useful if you want to protect your home for your children, provide for someone vulnerable, or make sure a life insurance payout reaches your family quickly. We will tell you honestly whether a trust would help you.
